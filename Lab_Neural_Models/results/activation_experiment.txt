--- Interpretation ---
All three activations solve XOR with the same seed and final losses of
order 1e-5, so this experiment does not crown a winner.  The local
derivative at step 0 clearly differs (sigmoid mean 0.245, tanh 0.920,
ReLU 1.000), so the activation does control the per-unit factor f'(a(1)).
The *norm* of the full first-layer gradient, however, is a composite:it multiplies the output-error signal, the output weights W(2), and
f'(a(1)), then sums over the four examples and both units.  Here those
composite norms land within ~10% of one another (0.0055, 0.0051,
0.0049), so one cannot read the activation's effect off the norm alone -
the derivative/pre-activation table above shows the mechanism directly.
Two distinct mechanisms can still kill a gradient: a saturated sigmoid
has f'(a) -> 0 because |a| is large, while a ReLU unit has f'(a) = 0
because a < 0.  They are told apart by inspecting the pre-activations
a(1) (large magnitude vs negative), not by the gradient alone.  With
four points and one seed these are observations, not a universal
ranking of activations.
